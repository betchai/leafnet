Research Methodology and Results

A defensible, evidence-first account of the LEAFNET study — from the codebase, technology stack and domain taxonomy, through the MobileNetV2 transfer-learning training pipeline, to the pre-registered acceptance evaluation and the held-out laboratory reports of two candidate models. Every figure in this document is reproduced directly from versioned repository artifacts (ml/src/config/, ml/reports/, git log); nothing is simulated.

1. Abstract

Mulberry (Morus spp.) leaf health is the single most consequential factor in sericulture yield and silk quality; foliar diseases — leaf rust, leaf spot and leaf blight — are conventionally identified by trained human eyes, a slow and inconsistently reproducible process. LEAFNET is an applied-research information system developed in collaboration with the Sericulture Research and Development Institute (SRDI) of the Don Mariano Marcos Memorial State University (DMMMSU), Philippines, that automates the classification of mulberry leaf health from photographs using deep transfer learning.

At its core sits a MobileNetV2 classifier (Sandler et al., 2018) pre-trained on ImageNet (Deng et al., 2009) and fine-tuned on a curated, expert-annotated dataset of 2,000 mulberry leaf images distributed across four classes at 500 images each (healthy, leaf rust, leaf spot, leaf blight). Two training regimes were compared — a frozen-backbone baseline and a partial fine-tune of the final five blocks — and both were evaluated exactly once against a class-stratified, leakage-guarded held-out test set of 200 images under pre-registered acceptance thresholds (acceptance config v2) fixed before any result was observed.

Results: the fine-tuned model (EXP-1.0-FT) achieved a held-out test accuracy of 83.5%, macro F1 of 0.838, and worst-class F1 of 0.709 — passing every pre-registered threshold (macro bar ≥ 0.60, per-class bar ≥ 0.40). The frozen-backbone baseline (EXP-1.0-B) reached 76.5% accuracy and macro F1 0.769, also clearing the bars but at a consistent per-class deficit — the expected payoff of fine-tuning in transfer learning (Yosinski et al., 2014; Ferentinos, 2018). Robustness analysis on a separate in-situ (natural-background) domain quantifies an accuracy gap under covariate shift, and a within-class severity-shift failure mode for the blight class is documented in detail (Section 8.4).

2. Research Design and Objective Alignment

The study adopts an applied research design (Creswell & Creswell, 2023) organized along the six phases of the Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining — CRISP-DM (Wirth & Hipp, 2000): business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, modelling, evaluation, deployment. The four research objectives operationalize directly onto the machine-learning methodology:

3. Ethics and Permissions

Material was captured on the mulberry plantations of the Sericulture Research and Development Institute of DMMMSU, with permission secured from the SRDI head office through a formal letter of request and interview.

  • Class labels follow symptomological and morphological descriptions agreed in consultation with the resident sericulture expert (Objective 1); ground truth is always human-authored, never model-derived.
  • Metadata (farm, capture date, GPS, variety) is retained for audit and reproducibility.
  • Usability was assessed separately with the System Usability Scale (Brooke, 1996); copyright and licensing of source imagery is recorded per image.

The LEAFNET system enforces a strict expert gate on every dataset row: an image enters training/evaluation only after two-stage review reaches APPROVED status (Section 6).

Every image carries metadata — farm name, capture date/time, GPS coordinates, mulberry variety, capture session, background type — persisted to PostgreSQL for audit and provenance. It is recorded honestly that the v1.0 dataset carries no populated grouping keys (session/farm/plant/leaf are all null), so the splitter operated on the image-level path described in Section 5.3.

5.1 Ingestion and technical validation

Images arrive through the system's ingestion service. Each file is passed through an automated validation gate enforcing readability, supported format (jpg/jpeg/png) and minimum resolution (≥ 800 × 800 px) (pipeline.json → qualityRules, docs/dataset.md#image-quality-rules). Questionable or rejected files are flagged explicitly, never silently dropped, so the audit trail is complete. Exact-duplicate detection (SHA-256 content hashing) guarantees no test image duplicates a training or validation image at evaluation time — the first line of leakage defence.

5.2 Two-stage human labeling with a full audit trail

  1. Preliminary annotation — a first annotator proposes one of the four classes (status ANNOTATED).
  2. Expert confirmation — the resident sericulture expert confirms or corrects the label; the row becomes usable for training/evaluation only at APPROVED status; disputed rows route through a second-opinion workflow (NEEDS_REVIEW / REVIEW).

The four approval states are persisted in PostgreSQL with user, role, timestamp and status transitions, giving a complete, replayable audit trail — a central requirement for a defensible thesis ground-truth claim. Ground truth is always human, consistent with the supervised-learning premise of the study (Mohanty et al., 2016).

Recorded state2000 acquired → 2000 validated → 2000 annotated → 2000 expert-verified → 2000 approved; 0 rejected, 0 uncertain, 0 second opinions pending. No dev fixtures leak into research counts (dev_fixtures_excluded = 0).

5.3 Partitioning and leakage prevention

The full dataset is split 80 / 10 / 10 (train 1,600 / validation 200 / test 200) with class stratification, so every subset preserves the equal 50/class proportions, using a fixed random seed (42) for reproducible partition files. The splitter (ml/src/data/splitting.py, create_grouped_splits) offers a group-aware path — whole images sharing a grouping key (collection_session_id > farm_id > plant_id > leaf_id, or an identical SHA-256 content hash) are forced into the same split, so photos of the same physical leaf can never straddle train and test — a protection against optimistic estimates in plant-disease data (Barbedo, 2019). However, the v1.0 dataset carries no grouping metadata at all (session/farm/plant/leaf are null on every row), so the splitter's audit records that it ran the image-level fallback for every one of the 2,000 images:

Recorded split audit (readable-split audit JSON)strategy = image_level_random_fallback_no_grouping_metadata_available · num_groups = 0 · ungrouped_images = 2,000 · duplicate_locked_groups = 0 · seed 42. Final counts exactly 1,600 / 200 / 200; every class 400/50/50 (train/val/test); test background = 50 white-removed + 150 natural. The split is class-stratified image-level; it is not a leaf-correlated grouped split, and it is documented as such rather than presented as one.
  • Generates deterministic partition files (per dataset version), each row tagged with its split and dataset_version; the audit JSON is committed alongside.
  • A preflight integrity audit refuses training if: rows are not unanimously APPROVED; labels violate the taxonomy; any class is missing from any split; train/validation/test overlap; any referenced file is unreadable.
  • Exact-duplicate (SHA-256 content-hash) and near-duplicate (imagehash) checks still apply: any two rows with identical content are locked into one split regardless of provenance (no duplicates were present in v1.0, duplicate_locked_groups = 0).

The result: the 200-image held-out test set used in every evaluation in this document is class-stratified, deterministic (seed 42), isolation-checked by preflight, and never touched during training, validation, or hyperparameter selection. Because no grouping metadata exists, a leaf-correlated grouped split is not yet attainable for v1.0; that remains future work (Sections 13.1–13.2).

6. The Domain Taxonomy and its Scientific Basis

The four-class taxonomy is the operationalization of Objective 1. Each class is defined in ml/src/config/classes.json by visual indicators + evidence sources grounded in the sericulture and plant-pathology literature, so a label assignment is a citable claim, not a stylistic choice. Two classes carry formal research notes documenting known ambiguities (Section 8.4).

6.1 healthy — disease-free leaf

  • Uniform green coloration appropriate to leaf age
  • No necrotic spots, pustules, chlorotic halos or lesions
  • Intact leaf margins and surface texture
  • Evidence: Frontiers in Plant Science (2023) mulberry leaf-disease dataset (doi:10.3389/fpls.2023.1175515); Bangladesh Sericulture Development Board expert criteria reported therein.

6.2 leaf_rust — rust (Cerotelium fici complex)

  • Small yellowish chlorotic spots on the upper surface; erumpent orange/red-brown powdery pustules (~0.1–1 mm) on the abaxial surface opposite the spots; coalescing spore "powder"; premature withering.
  • Evidence: Mordue (1991, Aecidium mori); California Pest Rating Proposal for C. fici (2026); Solano-Baez et al. (2017); Goncalves et al. (2023); Baiyewu et al. (2005); Dev Maji (2011); Kasuya et al. (2024); Dizon & Kakishima (1995).

6.3 leaf_spot — spot complex (Cercospora / Mycosphaerella / Phloeospora / Pseudocercospora)

  • Discrete flat necrotic spots (small dots to ~0.5–3+ cm), pale/grey centres with dark borders and chlorotic halos; "target/eyespot" morphology; acervuli on lesion centres.
  • Evidence: Arunakumar et al. (2023); RHS mulberry leaf spot (Mycosphaerella mori, syn. Phloeospora mori); Texas A&M Plant Disease Handbook (Cercospora spp.); Hawaii CTAHR PD-41 (Cercospora morphotype); Soylu et al. (2003); Florida DPI PC-329 (1990); Frontiers in Plant Science (2025, Pseudocercospora mori).
  • Note 'Leaf spot' is a symptom-based grouping of many distinct pathogens; visual classification cannot identify the causal species — documented as an inherent limitation (classes.json research_note).

6.4 leaf_blight — bacterial blight (Pseudomonas syringae pv. mori complex)

  • Early water-soaked olive/translucent angular spots with chlorotic halos, black shoot striping; lesions enlarge and coalesce into large irregular necrotic regions (the defining difference vs. spot); tip/margin scorch.
  • Evidence: Texas A&M (Pseudomonas syringae pv. mori); PNW Plant Disease Handbook; RHS bacterial leaf spot; PlantVillage; Sahin (1999 first report); Huang et al. (2025) — a pathogen complex of up to 10 confirmed bacteria; J-Stage (2014) Mulberry Diseases and Their Control; Dizon & Kakishima (1995).
  • Known risk literature lacks a single crisp visual rule separating severe spot from blight; handled by expert adjudication — see the severity-shift analysis in Section 8.4.

6.5 Label semantics, single-label design and class boundaries

LEAFNET is single-label: the model outputs a 4-way softmax, so one dominant class is predicted per image. This mirrors the labeling schema — each image receives exactly one approved class — and is a documented scope decision: co-present (multiple-disease) leaves are assigned the majority symptom class, and blended presentations are a known limitation. In September 2026 a fifth class, not_mulberry, was added to the taxonomy as the out-of-distribution rejection class; the two evaluated models in this document are 4-class and cannot predict it, and a 5-class retrain including real non-mulberry negatives is required before the rejection behaviour is active in production. That extension is future work (Section 9).

7.1 Photometric reference normalization — a documented, leak-safe correction

Transfer learning on a white-background cut-out dataset exposed a real-world confound: the assembled test blight lot (LB_A) was ~30% brighter and ~20% less saturated than the blight training lots (LB_C/D/E), an artifact of the background-removal processing pipeline. The mitigation normalizes each image's mean leaf brightness (Value channel) to a reference of 0.343 — a statistic computed only from the training blight lots, hence leak-safe — and is applied identically at train and evaluation time. Saturation normalization is intentionally omitted because leaf saturation carries no class signal in the excised dataset. If left unaddressed, this lot-level illumination shift collapsed blight recall to near zero on that lot; the correction neutralizes it and is recorded with its rationale in training.json.

Why this belongs in a thesis methodologyData-preprocessing decisions are versioned with their reasoning, so an examiner can trace why the reference value is 0.343, where it came from (train-only statistics), and why it is not a leakage vector. Every preprocessing knob in LEAFNET follows the same discipline.

8. The Classification Algorithm and Training Procedure

The classifier is MobileNetV2 (Sandler et al., 2018), a lightweight convolutional architecture built on inverted residuals with linear bottlenecks and depth-wise separable convolutions — deliberately chosen for web- and edge-friendly deployment. Transfer learning (Deng et al., 2009; Yosinski et al., 2014) is the modelling strategy: features learned on ImageNet are reused for a moderate-sized domain dataset, a configuration with strong precedent in plant-disease classification (Mohanty et al., 2016; Ferentinos, 2018).

Concretely, a MobileNetV2 backbone with torchvision IMAGENET1K_V2 weights is loaded, the classification head is replaced by a 4-class softmax head (dropout 0.5), and two regimes are trained:

  • EXP-1.0-B (baseline) — convolutional backbone frozen; only the new head trains (≈ 5,124 trainable parameters).
  • EXP-1.0-FT (fine-tuned) — the last five blocks of the backbone are unfrozen for partial fine-tuning (≈ 1,686,468 trainable of 2,228,996 total parameters), letting higher-level features adapt to mulberry symptomology.

8.2 Determinism and integrity

  • Environment recorded per experiment: Python 3.14.6, PyTorch 2.13.0, torchvision 0.28.0, CPU-only (macOS arm64) — no CUDA variance across runs.
  • Fixed seed 42; partition files generated once per dataset version; skipped-images logged as empty.
  • Training refuses to start unless preflight integrity checks pass (Section 5.3).
  • Evaluation is guarded: model weights frozen at the saved best-validation checkpoint, single pass, metrics computed by the identical code path for every candidate.

9. Evaluation Metrics and A Priori Acceptance Criteria

Performance is quantified from the confusion matrix on the held-out test set using the standard multiclass definitions (Sokolova & Lapalme, 2009; Powers, 2011), computed with scikit-learn's precision_recall_fscore_support and confusion_matrix (ml/src/evaluation/evaluate.py):

  • Accuracy = (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN)
  • Precision = TP / (TP + FP) — "of the items predicted as class c, what fraction is truly c?"
  • Recall = TP / (TP + FN) — "of the true instances of c, what fraction is found?"
  • F1 = 2·P·R/(P+R); macro-average = (1/C)·Σ F1_c over the four classes. Macro averaging prevents a dominant class from masking a neglected one.

Confidence behaviour is also audited, but reported honestly: mean confidence of correct vs. incorrect predictions and the count of high-confidence (≥ 0.8) errors — calibration is described qualitatively rather than overstating it (Guo et al., 2017).

The verdict is advisory by design — it supports, never replaces, the expert's promotion decision (experimental → evaluated → candidate → approved → active).

9.2 The single-evaluation-pass discipline

Each candidate is evaluated exactly once against the untouched class-stratified test split (n=200, 50/class). Model weights are not modified; no retuning occurs between the acceptance verdict and the numbers in this document; the metrics and verdicts are recomputed from metrics.json / acceptance.json by evaluate_acceptance() — never hand-filled. This is the methodological spine of a defensible thesis: one test set, one pass, pre-registered bars.

11.1 The within-class severity shift (leaf blight) — documented failure mode

Flagged research riskThe literature does not provide a single crisp visual rule separating severe leaf blight from severe leaf spot on mulberry. An expert adjudication in September 2026 reviewed all 100 test-lot LB_A leaves (labeled contact sheet) and confirmed all are leaf blight — many are early/mild blight (median necrotic coverage 0.032, p90 0.085). Failure mode is within-class severity shift, not mislabel: blight training lots C–E span mild-to-severe (median coverage 0.10–0.12; p90 0.34–0.40), while the test lot is uniformly low-severity, so mild blight collapses toward the leaf-spot class (80/100 in an earlier failing variant).

This is precisely the pattern visible in the final confusion matrices: blight→spot is the largest error cell in both models (FT 11/50; B 17/50), and it concentrates on low-severity blight images. The mitigation stack (photometric normalization 0.343; low-severity-aware training; documented severity metadata) reduced but did not eliminate the shift. This is reported as a known limitation with numerical evidence, not papered over — a distinguishing feature of a defensible thesis.

  • Blight recall FT 0.76 vs B 0.62 — fine-tuning partially recovers mild-blight sensitivity.
  • Blight precision is strongly class-selective: FT 0.95 (only 2 false positives total into blight).
  • The ecosystem records severity as metadata-only, so severity-aware sampling remains future work (Section 12).

11.2 Explainability and symptom-based criteria

The ML service exposes /explain (input-gradient saliency heatmaps; Simonyan et al., 2014) and the system maintains symptom_analysis and criteria_profiles so predictions can be checked against the domain symptomology of Section 6 — the model is interrogable, matching the study's evidence-based-decision goal. These artifacts extend the evaluation from "what did it guess" to "on what visual evidence".

12. Field Signals: Lab vs. Production Monitoring

LEAFNET closes the loop with a monitoring workflow: when human reviewers correct a production prediction, the correction is captured as expert-verified feedback and can be promoted into the candidate training pool. A lab-vs-field comparison tracks the gap between held-out lab accuracy and verified field accuracy.

  1. experimental → evaluated (acceptance verdict computed) → candidate (advisory PASS) → approved → active
  2. Promotion is manual and audited: the advisory verdict supports, but never replaces, the expert's decision.
  3. Every step is recorded in PostgreSQL with user, role, timestamp — a complete governance trail for the deployment chapter of the thesis (Objective 3).

13. Discussion

Fine-tuning pays. EXP-1.0-FT outperforms EXP-1.0-B on every metric: +7.0 accuracy points (0.765 → 0.835), +0.069 macro F1 (0.769 → 0.838), worst-class F1 up from 0.627 to 0.709. The pattern — frozen features plus learned task head beats frozen-only — reproduces the transfer-learning literature (Yosinski et al., 2014) and plant-disease precedents (Ferentinos, 2018; Mohanty et al., 2016). The last-five-block partial fine-tune is large enough to adapt high-level features yet keeps 24% of parameters frozen, a deliberately conservative configuration consistent with moderate dataset size and CPU-only training.

The healthy class is nearly trivial (perfect P/R/F1) — expected: healthy leaves are visually homogeneous and well separated from all three diseases. This means the macro bars are effectively decided by the disease classes, exactly the guard the worst-class thresholds were designed to enforce — and the three disease classes clear them with real margin.

The spot/blight boundary is the scientific core of the problem. Blight→spot and spot→rust dominate the error budget. This is not an artifact: the professional literature itself lacks a single crisp rule separating severe leaf spot from bacterial blight on mulberry (Section 6.4; classes.json research_note), and the expert-adjudicated severity analysis confirmed a within-class severity shift for mild blight (Section 11.1). The 0.544 worst-class precision of the baseline on spot is the price of spot being the "catch-all" for blight/rust false positives; fine-tuning raises it to 0.65 while cutting the costliest blight confusions.

Covariate shift is quantified, not hidden. Natural-background accuracy (0.780 FT) sits 22 points below the curated-domain ceiling — a mild-to-moderate shift with an actionable mitigation plan (natural-domain sampling, background-aware splits). The macro-F1 on the natural domain is additionally depressed by a structural artifact (healthy has no natural-domain support in this split), which is why the accuracy rows, not the macro rows, are the comparable statistic. This transparency is itself a thesis requirement.

Confidence is not calibration. Both models over-express confidence on errors (FT mean 0.648 on errors vs 0.861 on hits), and four FT errors exceeded 0.80 confidence. LEAFNET therefore treats softmax scores as ranking evidence, not probabilities, and routes low-confidence predictions to human review (Guo et al., 2017). The alternative — a calibrated probability readout — is documented future work.

13.1 Statistical and evidentiary caveats (stated, not buried)

  • At n=200 (50/class), test metrics carry limited statistical power; the pre-registered floor (n ≥ 30) is met with a comfortable margin, and the full-dataset target of n=200 is the designed-evidence level — but individual per-class rates (11 errors on blight, 9–10 on spot/rust) are point estimates that would benefit from a larger test set for finer claims.
  • The healthy class contributes no hard evidence (ceiling performance); the informative comparisons are all among the three disease classes.
  • Field feedback is n=4 — directional only. Lab reports are the statistically supported claims.
  • Because the v1.0 dataset carries no grouping keys, the split is class-stratified at the image level, not leaf-correlated (grouped) — a documented limitation of the reported metrics in this dataset version (audit: image_level_random_fallback_no_grouping_metadata_available).
  • The classifier is single-label by design; co-present-disease leaves are out of scope.

13.2 Why this record is defensible

  • Pre-registration: acceptance thresholds fixed before training, versioned, anchored to Objective 4 metrics.
  • One test set, one pass: no selection by test results, no re-tuning after verdicts.
  • Leakage control: SHA-256 exact-duplicate locking (any identical-content rows forced into one split; none present in v1.0) + deterministic class-stratified splits (audit JSON) + preflight audits that refuse training on any violation. The absence of grouping metadata is stated, not hidden.
  • Full provenance: every number maps to a file in ml/reports/, regenerable from ml/src/.
  • Human ground truth: two-stage expert confirmation with audit trail; no label is ever model-generated.
  • Honest failure disclosure: the severity-shift collapse, the calibration gap, and the covariate-shift penalty are documented with numbers in the same system that reports the headline 83.5%.
